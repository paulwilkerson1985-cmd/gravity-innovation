# Widening the case: which theories a ≥1 m open-vacuum torsion test constrains

*Fable 5 · 26 Sep 2026 · Gravity Innovation · companion: `model_reach.py`, `model_reach_output.txt`, `dilaton_reach.py`, `dilaton_reach.out/.json` (dilaton solved on the validated cut-cell grid); referee notes in `../referee1_screened.md`*

Benchmark used throughout: Cu spheres 1 kg (R = 3 cm) and 8.1 kg (R = 6 cm), 14 cm apart, 1 m chamber; F_N = 2.8e-8 N; Tier 1 = 3e-11 N, Tier 2 = 3e-13 N. Estimates are point-like Yukawa (conservative by <=1.8x versus the validated solver's finite-size factor).

## 1. Other screened / environment-dependent models

**Bottom line.** The "needs a macroscopic open vacuum to switch on" feature is specific to Z2-breaking (symmetron-type) fields. Chameleons and dilatons are *chamber-size-dependent* (power law, no threshold) and a 1 m chamber does help them, but their reachable corners are either already closed (n = 1 chameleon at Lambda_DE) or unmapped (dilaton at TeV-scale A2). Disformal, quadratic-ULDM, screened-vector and Vainshtein models add nothing. The clean widenings are: **any symmetron-class model (generalized coupling/potential, radiatively generated, "asymmetron") with 1/mu = 2–30 cm**; **the environment-dependent dilaton at TeV-scale M_eff, where Tier 2 excludes 2.5 decades of lambda (5e25–2.5e28 at V0 = rho_Lambda) that no published bound resolves**; plus a by-product improvement of plain Yukawa bounds at 10–30 cm.

### 1b. Two additions from the referee pass (`../referee1_screened.md`, `../neff_estimate.py`)

- **Positioning against Clements et al. 2024** (PRD 109, 123023, arXiv:2308.01179 — Burrage's group): a 10 cm UHV chamber in which the symmetron condenses by the same density-driven transition, probed with atoms/nanobeads at 1/μ = 1–10 mm, M = 0.1–10 TeV, λ ~ 1e-10, with domain walls as the *signal*. Our proposal is its macroscopic-mass extension one decade up in range (1/μ = 2–30 cm), where the screened-body force is M-independent; trapped walls (`u9`: none metastable at R = 5/μ, present at R ≥ 8/μ) are an observable, not only a systematic. This is the single most important framing change for the Nottingham email.
- **Thermal-relic ΔN_eff bound on the benchmark** (new; `neff_estimate_output.txt`): in the symmetric phase the field is light and thermalises via the T^μ_μ coupling; decoupling at T_dec ≈ 65 / 100 / 130 / 195 / ≥250 MeV for M = 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / ≥6 TeV gives ΔN_eff ≈ 0.39 / 0.39 / 0.30 / 0.06 / 0.056 (0.04 at M ≥ 36 TeV). Against Planck+BAO (≲0.3) and BBN+CMB (≲0.15–0.2): **M ≲ 4 TeV excluded, 4–6 TeV marginal (QCD-crossover sensitive), ≥6 TeV allowed; CMB-S4 (σ ≈ 0.03) would probe the whole wedge.** This is an independent ~5 TeV floor that no nuclear-physics uncertainty can move, and it makes the wedge read "cosmology below, air above, HUST-09 at the top". Needs a proper hadron-resonance-gas + lattice-trace calculation before it is quoted as a bound (Weinberg 2013; Brust–Kaplan–Walters 2013; Yeh et al. 2022).

## 2. Naturalness

The tuning is real and should be stated with numbers, not adjectives. The operator phi^2 T/2M^2 gives delta mu^2 ~ Lambda_UV^4/(16 pi^2 M^2): with M = 5 TeV, mu = 1e-5 eV the required cancellation in mu^2 is 1e-19 (cutoff 1 GeV), 1e-28 (200 GeV), 1e-31 (1 TeV). The transparent UV completion is a Higgs portal lambda_p phi^2 |H|^2 with lambda_p = m_h^2/2M^2 ≈ 3e-4, which induces m_phi ≈ 4 GeV versus the 1e-5 eV wanted (1e-30). The quartic lambda = mu^2/v^2 ≈ 3e-10 is small but not itself a problem.

Mitigation routes that exist in print:
- **Radiative generation** (BCM 2016, arXiv:1604.06051): the symmetron potential is the one-loop Coleman–Weinberg potential of the matter coupling, so loops are the model rather than a correction; but the viable region is M ~ 1e-5 M_Pl, not TeV.
- **Scale invariance / Higgs portal** (Burrage–Copeland–Millington–Spannowsky 2018, arXiv:1804.07180): conformally coupled scalars are Higgs-portal theories; tree-level fifth forces appear only through mass mixing and their size tracks explicit vs. spontaneous scale breaking. This gives a language ("the fifth-force coupling measures explicit scale breaking") but no natural ueV/TeV point.
- **Quantum corrections to the profile itself** (arXiv:2508.16726, 2025) find the symmetron force may be weaker than the classical profile — relevant to the *prediction*, not the tuning.
- Shift symmetry, sequestering, "dark" partial couplings: no published route gives ueV with TeV coupling; a coupling to electrons only would need M_e ~ 80 GeV, excluded at LEP.

Recommended wording: "The ueV mass with TeV-scale coupling is radiatively unstable at the 1e-20–1e-30 level, as is every laboratory symmetron and chameleon (Upadhye–Hu–Khoury 2012). We treat the model as a phenomenological parametrization of a documented experimental blind spot, in the same spirit as the atom-interferometer programme; the honest prior is set by this tuning, and we quote it."

## 3. Links to open questions — strict answers

- **Big G.** No. Every experiment in the CODATA spread has the source outside the vacuum or behind a shield, so the field is off in all of them and cannot produce *different* biases; the one partial exception (HUST-09) sets a bound, it does not explain a spread. The ~500 ppm scatter is a metrology problem (Rothleitner & Schlamminger 2017; Li et al. 2018).
- **Wide binaries / MOND.** No. The field is short-range (<= 30 cm) and its scales have nothing to do with a0; in the interstellar medium it sits in its broken phase and mediates nothing at parsec scales.
- **DESI evolving dark energy.** No. The vacuum mass sqrt(2) mu ≈ 1e-5 eV >> H0, so the field has been frozen at its minimum since 1+z ~ 1e8–1e9. Its vacuum energy -mu^2 v^2/4 is a constant absorbed in Lambda. There is a numerical coincidence worth one sentence: for detectable v^2 ~ 0.4 eV^2 and 1/mu = 2–10 cm, |V_min| = 0.01–0.3 rho_Lambda. It explains nothing and should not be sold as motivation.
- **Hubble tension.** No. The Z2 transition happens at T ~ 100–200 keV (tail of BBN) but releases ~1e-13 eV^4 against rho_rad ~ 1e20 eV^4; the nucleon-mass shift in the broken phase is v^2/2M^2 <~ 1e-19. No N_eff, no early dark energy.

## 4. What a positive result would enable (sober version)

A detection would be a new, composition-independent, density-gated force: a Nobel-class result in fundamental physics. The milestone sequence is (1) replication by a second group with a different chamber; (2) the fingerprint programme turned into measurements: M from foil thickness, mu from the distance law and chamber threshold, lambda (equivalently v) from the absolute force; (3) the atom-interferometer cross-check (unscreened probe, ∝ 1/M^2); (4) laboratory symmetry-breaking physics: domain walls, hysteresis of the gas switch, the field's own excitations at ~1e-5 eV (GHz) — a table-top analogue of a cosmological phase transition; (5) then cosmology: was the field ever relevant (BCKM 2019 symmetron dark matter), and a collider search for the Higgs-portal completion at lambda_p ~ 1e-4.

Engineering: the force in the entire allowed band is attractive, two-body, <= 1e-5 N (a milligram-weight) between kg masses, and exists only inside a vacuum larger than ~3.3/mu. Earth's field is pinned by rock and air, so there is nothing to push against; propulsion is not on any horizon. Realistic uses are metrological: a switchable, calculable, composition-blind reference force for torsion balances and gradiometers, and a density-threshold sensor (the field turns off at 0.585 rho_crit). Anything beyond that requires a *different* field with a stronger v, for which there is no evidence.

## 5. Recommendation

Reframe from "ueV symmetron" to the class the experiment actually tests, with the symmetron as the benchmark, and add the two honest widenings (dilaton corner, Yukawa by-product). Do not claim chameleon reach as new space.

**Title:** *A macroscopic-mass test of density-gated scalar fields in a metre-scale open vacuum*

**Abstract (<=150 words):** Scalar fields whose vacuum value depends on the size of the empty region around them — symmetrons and their generalizations, environment-dependent dilatons, chameleons — have been tested with atoms, neutrons and sub-millimetre torsion balances, all inside chambers of radius <~ 6 cm or with sources outside the vacuum or behind shields. Any such field with a vacuum range above ~2 cm therefore never switches on in existing apparatus. We propose a torsion balance with kg-scale source and test masses sharing a >= 1 m unshielded vacuum. For the benchmark symmetron (1/mu = 2–30 cm, M = 4–100 TeV) the screened-body force is independent of M, and a set of environmental switch tests — chamber liner, gas density, foil thickness, composition, distance law — gives a signature no artifact shares. The same measurement excludes the environment-dependent dilaton over 5e25 < lambda < 2.5e28 at TeV-scale effective coupling and improves inverse-square-law limits at 10–30 cm tenfold. A null result closes a documented gap.
